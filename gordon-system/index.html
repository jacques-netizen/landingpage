    02 · Content System

    LinkedIn, 4–5 posts a week.

    2a · The anchor framework

    Reverse-engineered from Lara Acosta’s live feed, then adapted for Gordon’s older corporate audience.

    1. Campaign architecture, not a content calendar. During a launch window, every post, regardless of type, funnels to one destination and one CTA. Multiple angles, one door.
    2. Cadence: 4–5 posts per week.
    3. The repeating post skeleton:
      1. Hook: a specific number or a vulnerability admission. Never a topic statement.
      2. Short one-to-three line stanzas, heavy white space.
      3. A negation or contrast block mid-post (“I don’t X, you don’t need Y”; “How-to sounds generic, how-I stops the scroll”).
      4. Numbered steps or one compressed lesson.
      5. Visual proof attached to every post: analytics screenshots, receipts of old posts, event photos, video.
      6. A CTA with a hard date, then a PS/PPS engineered for engagement: a question, a repost ask, an open loop.
    4. SLAY structure underlies the story format: Story → Lesson → Actionable advice → You (question or CTA), roughly 200 words.

    Six rotating post angles:

    Client proof with receipts Vulnerability / origin story with receipts Money or result proof, negation stack, steps Craft tip: never do X, do this instead Tool tutorial, numbered walkthrough Why / emotional story tied to a number

    Topic sourcing wells: problems solved for self, problems solved for clients, feed takes to agree or disagree with, and reverse-engineering his own best performers monthly.

    2b · Gordon’s adaptation rules
    • RegisterSame skeleton, senior register. No cringe or slay vocabulary, no bro-speak. Hooks written for a fifty to sixty year old founder or executive.
    • Vulnerability angleCareer-honesty stories: what he got wrong across decades of marketing, moments of reinvention. Not student-hustle stories.
    • Proof assets he already ownsPodcast clips and guest moments from the Influence Global Podcast, client reviews, speaking and event photos, the book. Every post carries one.
    • Authority data as his analytics screenshotCreator-economy data (for example, the $250bn creator economy) plays the role Lara’s analytics screenshots play, paired with a client story wherever possible.
    • The belief to attack repeatedly“Social media doesn’t sell products or people.” This is his ICP’s stated limiting belief.
    • Launch-mode optionWhen Gordon has a date-bound push (VIP intake, Top 100 Index, AI Hackathon, a workshop), the whole week’s posts flip to single-CTA campaign mode, Lara-style.
