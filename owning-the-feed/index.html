01 · Summary

The campaign has said it intends to win the social media war.

The question is no longer whether to fight online. It is who can turn that intent into daily, measurable reach before campaigning closes.

The race is moving. Since May, the President's net favourability has climbed from -58.5 to -3.3, the sharpest shift of any candidate in the field. One gap has not closed: young voters in the cities. They favour the main opposition ticket by more than two to one, and they are the group that decides who wins the online conversation.

That conversation is still unowned. The opposition is loud but runs on unpaid volunteers who post in bursts. The campaign's own assets, the City Boy Movement's ward structures, the planned 4,000-influencer network and the campaign council's youth framework, are strong on the ground but have no engine to keep them supplied with content every day.

We are that engine. We take every speech, interview and moment of the President, cut it into thousands of short videos, and distribute them daily through a large network of Nigerian creators on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and Facebook, in English, Pidgin, Yoruba and Hausa. The same content feeds the influencer network, the City Boy Movement and WhatsApp.

02 · The online race today

A three-way contest, with a split opposition.

The field

INEC has published a final list of 18 presidential candidates. The contest that matters is three-way: the President and Vice President Shettima for the APC, Atiku Abubakar and Rotimi Amaechi for the ADC, and Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso for the NDC. The opposition failed to unite behind one ticket. Its vote is split.

Where sentiment stands

The most rigorous public voter tracker, published 21 September 2026, shows a race that is narrowing.

The economy is the tailwind. Inflation has fallen from 34.8% in December 2024 to 15.39% in August 2026. Growth reached 4.43% in the second quarter. Reserves passed USD 52 billion, and all three major rating agencies have upgraded Nigeria. Petrol remains the sharpest grievance, and food prices are still rising at close to 20% a year.

The youth gap

Almost 6.9 million new voters registered for 2027, and about two thirds of them are aged 18 to 34. These voters did not live through the fuel subsidy debate as policy. They lived it as prices. They rarely watch the news. They see politics on their phones, between football, music and comedy.

In 2023, this group made the opposition's online movement. It won the online conversation outright and carried Lagos, and still finished third nationally. The lesson runs both ways: online dominance alone does not win elections, but a campaign that cedes the online space lets the other side define it.

It is also the opportunity. The campaign does not have to lose online.

An influencer network with nothing new to post goes quiet. Ward coordinators need something to share every morning. Announcements do not become reach on their own. What is missing is an engine that does four things.

  1. Turns every appearance by the President into a steady stream of short videos, every day, until 15 January.

  2. Puts those videos on hundreds of thousands of feeds through creators with real audiences.

  3. Hands the same material to the influencer network, the City Boy Movement and WhatsApp groups, ready to post.

  4. Measures what is working, state by state and language by language, and adjusts weekly.

04 · The playbook

Two campaigns that became impossible to avoid.

Case study 01 · United States, 2024

The Trump campaign and the clip layer

Young men 56% to 42%
The problem
In 2020, young American voters backed the Democratic candidate by about 25 points. The campaign concluded that people who watch cable news had already made up their minds. The persuadable voters, especially young men, were not watching the news at all.
The move
Over the summer and autumn of 2024, the candidate sat down for nearly a dozen long-form conversations on podcasts and streams that rarely cover politics: Joe Rogan, Theo Von, Logan Paul, the Nelk Boys and the streamer Adin Ross. These were not policy interviews. One adviser described it as a numbers game: an hour of conversation with someone who reaches 15 million people is worth more than any rally.
The scale
He joined TikTok on 1 June 2024 at a UFC event, passed a million followers within hours, and his first video drew more than 24 million views. The Adin Ross livestream peaked above 500,000 live viewers. The Joe Rogan interview drew nearly 30 million YouTube views in its first 24 hours. Allied influencer operations claimed a combined reach of more than 140 million people.
Joe Rogan Experience #2219, Donald Trump
The source appearance · three hours · nearly 30M views in 24 hours

That is the source. The clip layer is what made him inescapable: every long-form appearance cut into thousands of short clips by the campaign's own accounts, allied accounts and fans, then posted across TikTok, Reels, Shorts and X for weeks. Coverage at the time called 2024 the clippers' election. Moments were built to be clipped: the fryer at a McDonald's in Pennsylvania, the garbage truck the day after an opponent's remark. Each travelled far beyond anyone who watched live.

The result
Young men backed him 56% to 42%. The Democrats' lead among all voters aged 18 to 29 collapsed from about 25 points in 2020 to about 4.
Why it worked, and its limit
It reached voters who ignore political media, it showed a person rather than a platform, and it faced almost no competition in that space. By 2026 his approval among young voters had fallen sharply. Attention wins the campaign. Delivery keeps it.
Case study 02 · New York, 2025

The Mamdani campaign and daily vertical video

Won, outspent throughout
The problem
Zohran Mamdani entered the New York mayoral race as a little-known state assemblyman polling in single digits. His main rival was a former governor with name recognition, establishment backing and a super PAC that spent more than USD 28 million on his behalf.
The move
Instead of trying to match that money, the campaign built a small in-house video team that published short vertical videos every day. The breakout format started after the 2024 election: the candidate on Fordham Road in the Bronx, holding a sign and asking people who had voted Trump why. His creative director summed up the philosophy: the style is downstream from the substance.
The scale
One video reached 4.5 million views on Instagram, 56% of them from people who did not follow him. A video about Manhattan traffic reached 2.3 million, almost half from non-followers. The campaign's stated goal: if you are on your phone in New York City, you are going to see him.

The multiplier
A creator network took the content further. Creators with anywhere from 20,000 to 35 million followers opted in through automated message flows. The campaign turned 45,000 social media messages into volunteers on the ground.
Why it worked
Short-form reach outran paid advertising because it was daily, human and native to the platforms. And the online reach did not stay online. It fed a volunteer army that knocked doors.

What this means for Nigeria

  1. Go where the voters are, not where the media is. TikTok, Reels, Shorts and WhatsApp, not X and television.

  2. Show the person. Long, relaxed conversations produce the moments that travel.

  3. Build the clip layer. One appearance becomes thousands of clips across thousands of accounts.

  4. Be there every day. Presence has to be constant, not event-driven.

  5. Connect online reach to the ground. Nigeria already has the ground structure in the City Boy Movement. What it needs is the clip layer.

05 · The strategy

Three storylines. Every clip serves one.

Storyline 01

The hard call is paying off

The President took decisions others avoided, and the results are arriving: inflation down by more than half from its peak, growth rising, reserves at their highest in years. Told honestly, with respect for what people went through, never pretending things are easy.

Storyline 02

It is working for people like me

1.39 million students have received government student loans. Their stories, in their own words and their own languages, are more persuasive than any statistic or minister. The same goes for every programme with real beneficiaries.

Storyline 03

The man behind the office

Humour, family, Lagos, football, music, history. Most young Nigerians have never seen the President unscripted. Every relaxed moment is content.

The source material

Every national address, interview and appearance is raw material. Each one below is a full-length broadcast that the engine turns into hundreds of vertical clips, in four languages, on the same day it airs.

Content supply, integration and response

What we recommend filming
  • At least one relaxed long-form conversation each month on a show young Nigerians already watch. Each one feeds the network for weeks.
  • A beneficiary series filmed across the regions: students, traders, workers, in their own words.
  • Capture of every campaign event, rally and City Boy Movement gathering, shot with short-form in mind.
Where it goes each day
  • Ready-to-post packs for the 4,000-influencer network.
  • City Boy Movement coordinators at state and ward level.
  • WhatsApp distribution, where 96.5% of Nigerian internet users already are.
  • When an attack lands, the answer is a clip, not a press statement. Factual, calm, on feeds within hours.
What we will not do

We only use footage and talking points the campaign approves. No fabricated content, no fake accounts, no attacks on ordinary citizens or protesters.

In a campaign this visible, one exposed fake would become the story. Our discipline protects the campaign.

Before launch we measure the online race as it stands: how often each candidate is seen, where, in which language, and with what sentiment. From then on the campaign receives a live dashboard and a weekly report on share of voice against the other candidates, sentiment by region and language, the moments that travelled furthest, and what we are changing as a result. No public poll tracks the online race. The campaign will have its own scoreboard.
