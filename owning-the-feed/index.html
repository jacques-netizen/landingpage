01 · Summary

The campaign has said it intends to win the social media war.

The question is no longer whether to fight online. It is who can turn that intent into daily, measurable reach before campaigning closes.

The race is moving. Since May, the President's net favourability has climbed from -58.5 to -3.3, the sharpest shift of any candidate in the field. One gap has not closed: young voters in the cities. They favour the main opposition ticket by more than two to one, and they are the group that decides who wins the online conversation.

That conversation is still unowned. The opposition is loud but runs on unpaid volunteers who post in bursts. The City Boy Movement's ward structures, the planned 4,000-influencer network and the campaign council's youth framework are strong on the ground, but none of them has an engine keeping it supplied with content every day.

We are that engine. We take every speech, interview and moment of the President, cut it into thousands of short videos, and distribute them daily through a large network of Nigerian creators on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and Facebook. The same content feeds the influencer network and the City Boy Movement.

The problem
In 2020, young American voters backed the Democratic candidate by about 25 points. The campaign concluded that people who watch cable news had already made up their minds. The persuadable voters, especially young men, were not watching the news at all.
The move
Nearly a dozen long-form conversations on podcasts and streams that rarely cover politics: Joe Rogan, Theo Von, Logan Paul, the Nelk Boys, Adin Ross. Not policy interviews. One adviser called it a numbers game: an hour with someone who reaches 15 million people is worth more than any rally.
1.6M views
@trumpclipsusa
A clip account, not the campaign · 55.6K likes

This is what made him inescapable. That account is not the campaign. It is one of thousands that cut every long-form appearance into short clips and posted them across TikTok, Reels, Shorts and X for weeks.

Coverage at the time called 2024 the clippers' election. Moments were built to be clipped: the fryer at a McDonald's in Pennsylvania, the garbage truck the day after an opponent's remark.

The limit: by 2026 his approval among young voters had fallen sharply. Attention wins the campaign. Delivery keeps it.

The problem
Zohran Mamdani entered the New York mayoral race as a little-known state assemblyman polling in single digits. His main rival was a former governor with name recognition, establishment backing and a super PAC that spent more than USD 28 million on his behalf.
The move
Instead of matching that money, the campaign built a small in-house team that published short vertical videos every day. The breakout format: the candidate on Fordham Road in the Bronx with a sign, asking people who had voted Trump why. His creative director: the style is downstream from the substance.
256K views
@sidetalknyc
An independent NYC clip account · 19.6K likes

The reach did not come from his own page. Sidetalk is one of New York's biggest street-content accounts, and it put him in front of an audience that follows basketball and the city, not politics.

A creator network took the content further: creators from 20,000 to 35 million followers opted in through automated message flows. The goal, in the campaign's words: if you are on your phone in New York City, you are going to see him.

And the online reach did not stay online. It fed a volunteer army that knocked doors.

What this means for Nigeria

Go where the voters are. TikTok, Reels and Shorts, not X and television.

Show the person. Relaxed conversations produce the moments that travel.

Build the clip layer. One appearance becomes thousands of clips.

Be there every day. Constant presence, not event-driven.

Connect it to the ground. The City Boy Movement is the ground. It needs the clip layer.

05 · The strategy

Three storylines. Every clip serves one.

01

The hard call is paying off

The President took decisions others avoided, and the results are arriving: inflation down by more than half from its peak, growth rising, reserves at their highest in years. Told honestly, with respect for what people went through.

02

It is working for people like me

1.39 million students have received government student loans. Their stories, in their own words, are more persuasive than any statistic or minister. The same goes for every programme with real beneficiaries.

03

The man behind the office

Humour, family, Lagos, football, music, history. Most young Nigerians have never seen the President unscripted. Every relaxed moment is content.

Supply and distribution

What we recommend filming
  • At least one relaxed long-form conversation each month on a show young Nigerians already watch. Each one feeds the network for weeks.
  • A beneficiary series filmed across the regions: students, traders, workers, in their own words.
  • Every campaign event, rally and City Boy Movement gathering, shot with short-form in mind.
Where it goes each day
  • Ready-to-post packs for the 4,000-influencer network.
  • City Boy Movement coordinators at state and ward level.
  • When an attack lands, the answer is a clip, not a press statement. Factual, calm, on feeds within hours.
What we will not do

We only use footage and talking points the campaign approves. No fabricated content, no fake accounts, no attacks on ordinary citizens or protesters.

In a campaign this visible, one exposed fake would become the story. Our discipline protects the campaign.

06 · The machine

Built for this job, paid on results.

~3,000
The network

Clippers today, expanded for this campaign with established Nigerian creators selected for real audiences, plus Nigerians abroad who shape conversation at home.

The platform

Creator sign-up and vetting, a library of approved footage, submission and review, view tracking and payouts, in one place.

Paid on results

Creators are paid only for verified views. No views, no pay. Botted views, recycled accounts and off-message content are rejected and not paid.

Reporting

A live dashboard, open to the campaign at any time: views, clips, platforms, states and the best-performing moments.

Before launch we measure the online race as it stands: how often each candidate is seen, where, and with what sentiment. From then on, a weekly report on share of voice against the other candidates, sentiment by region, and the moments that travelled furthest. No public poll tracks the online race. The campaign will have its own scoreboard.
