01 · Mandate and role

A revenue and growth role, not a content role.

03 · Positioning and messaging

Simple, outcome-led, and inside the guardrails.

Positioning and messaging are owned and authored by the EVP and finalized against the real product once access is granted. This keeps the public message simple and outcome-led, and keeps it inside the protected-methods guardrails. For each product, the messaging framework defines four things:

Component 1
Positioning & core promise

A positioning statement and core promise in plain language. What the product does for a person, said the way a person would say it.

Component 2
Persona-to-angle map

A map of personas to message angles across the doctrine's audiences, so every creator and asset speaks to a specific person, not the average of all of them.

Component 3
Proof approach

How savings, recoveries, and outcomes are shown: as examples, and where tracked, as data. Claims stand on evidence.

Component 4
The "do not say" guide

A list of the language creators and content must avoid. The framework commits to the method and the guardrails, not to draft copy.

04 · Growth model

Creator-led distribution, in six stages.

Both products share a clear consumer outcome: a charge caught before it billed, or money recovered that was owed. That outcome is well suited to short-form content and creator demonstration. The model produces the impression volume the funnel requires at a cost that scales with results rather than with spend committed up front.

Source

A small number of high-quality assets: real reviews from mid-tier YouTubers, founder podcast appearances, and genuine save-and-recover stories.

Multiply

Each long-form asset is cut into 30 to 50 short vertical clips, producing hundreds of assets from a small number of sources.

Distribute

A performance-paid clipper network posts the clips at volume. This is the primary source of impressions.

Amplify

Paid spend is applied only behind clips that have already proven they convert.

Capture

Waitlists and landing pages turn attention into an owned list.

Convert

Email sequences and the launch push turn the list into paying users.

05 · Creator and distribution program

A performance channel and a trust channel at once.

The program runs across the doctrine's creator categories, and all creators operate on tracked links and codes.

Savings creators Family creators Student creators Business creators Travel creators Productivity creators
Recruitment model

The launch recruits the team

The launch campaign also functions as the recruitment process. Open clip bounties and creator slots surface the strongest performers through their actual view and conversion numbers. The best clippers, UGC creators, and bloggers are then brought onto the internal team on a commission basis, paid on the results they drive.

Creator rules, enforced

Four non-negotiables

Approved product names only, with no description of confidential internal methods. Every creator on a tracking link or code, with attribution recorded per person from the start. Any content involving money, savings, refunds, or outcomes is reviewed before publication. Compensation accounts for deliverables, usage rights, exclusivity, and performance.

07 · Content engine

Multiplication, not manual volume.

The pre-launch content targets are met through multiplication rather than manual volume. A small set of hero sources is cut into hundreds of clips and then expanded by the creator network. All content types are drawn from the same pipeline, and all of it runs on an approved calendar across the launch channels so that no channel goes live empty.

10 · Funnel logic and targets

Explicit about which part of the funnel marketing owns.

The doctrine's funnel is a planning discipline: attention to visits to signups to paid.

Marketing owns
Impressions → Visits → Signups

Produced through clips and creators, and then measured.

Product owns
Signup → Paid → Retained

These depend on product quality, pricing, and onboarding. Marketing supports them with data and feedback.

Discipline

Prove, then scale

The funnel is validated on a measured first cohort before spend is scaled. Once the converting messages and channels are proven, spend is increased behind them.

When conversion runs below plan

The response is a list, not a panic

Improve message clarity, landing pages, onboarding, creator fit, pricing, trust proof, email nurturing, and retargeting, consistent with the doctrine.

11 · Reporting and accountability

Measured from impression to purchase.

Reach

Impressions, reach, follower growth, video views, watch time.

Traffic

Landing visits, unique visitors, source and channel, click-through rate.

Acquisition

Waitlist signups, installs, account creations, trial starts.

Conversion

Paid users, checkout completion, purchases.

Economics

Revenue by product, acquisition cost, payback, creator and channel ROI.

Retention & proof of value

Active users, churn, renewals avoided, refunds recovered.

Cadence: a weekly executive KPI report, a monthly performance report, and an attribution report, all maintained on a live dashboard.

12 · Roadmap

Foundation is built once. Then the engine operates.

Foundation

Brand systems for both products, channels, waitlists, email and tracking, first hero assets, and the creator brief.

Audience

Open the clipper campaign, test hooks, and recruit the creator bench.

Demand

Scale the winning clips, convert top talent to commission, and finalize landing pages.

Launch

Clips, creators, email sequences, referral push, and daily reporting.

Optimize

Focus on the winners, fix onboarding, and build retention and case studies.

Phase 1 is complete when
  • Channels are operational and branded, with visual identity standards set
  • The content library and an approved calendar exist
  • The creator program is active and community building has begun
  • Launch assets are prepared

No major paid acquisition begins until Phase 1 is complete.
