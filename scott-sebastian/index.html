01 · Where things stand

You have already done the hardest part.

Since 2021, word of mouth alone has carried the brand from friends in Hamburg to strangers stopping you in supermarkets and gyms to ask what you are wearing. Large-follower fragrance influencers are reaching out unprompted. That is not a marketing achievement. That is product-market fit most brands spend years chasing.

Already proven
  • Four years of organic word of mouth, with no paid distribution behind it.
  • Strangers asking about the scent in public, unprompted.
  • Fragrance influencers approaching you, rather than the other way round.
  • Demand outpacing what you currently produce.
Not yet attached to anything
  • Someone smells it, loves it, and has no way to trace that moment back to you. No story, no name, no packaging worth keeping.
  • No tracked CAC, click-through or conversion data, so nothing tells you what is working.
  • Packaging does not yet reflect the quality of what is inside, down to print that rubs off the bottle with handling.

None of that is a flaw in the brand. It is a gap between what the product has already proven and what the market has been shown, and closing that gap is what this proposal is for.

The core shift

Right now the story is that a few guys make good perfume. The story with leverage is that demand for a limited, artisan-made fragrance has already outpaced supply, before a single dollar of paid distribution was spent.

That is the version that supports premium pricing, earns press and shelf conversations with names like Harrods and Selfridges, and gives influencers something worth talking about beyond another new fragrance.

02 · The approach

Two phases, in sequence.

You said it yourself: you want this to be an asset you are proud of at 65, not a hobby that got lucky. That kind of longevity is not built by running paid distribution before the story is set. Positioning first, then distribution, so every dollar of visibility lands on something that converts and compounds.
