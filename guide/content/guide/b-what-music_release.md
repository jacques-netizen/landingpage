A song grows when people hear it again and again, in many places, in a short window. One post from one account is easy to scroll past. The same song under twenty different clips, on twenty different accounts, is hard to miss.

Clippers cut short videos that use your track: a live moment, a lyric that hits, a dance, a reaction, a behind the scenes moment. They post them on their own accounts, where the feed shows them to people who have never heard of you. When people hear the hook enough times, they search for it, add it to playlists and start using the sound in their own posts. That is when a release starts to move on its own.

### What makes music clips work

**The hook comes first.** Clips that open on the strongest eight seconds of the song do better than clips that build up to it.

**The real audio matters.** When clips use your official sound, every clip adds to the sound's count on the platform, and people know exactly what to search for.

**Timing matters.** Clips work hardest in the first weeks after release, while the song is new and people are deciding what to play.
