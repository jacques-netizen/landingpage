The part nobody else mentions

You will not pay for views that aren't real

The fastest way for an agency to flatter a report is to let bots inflate the numbers. We treat view-fraud as a design problem. Payout caps, manual review of every clip, and active monitoring mean your budget pays for genuine reach.

It is also why brands come back. A campaign you can trust is a campaign you scale.

Pricing has two parts. A retainer covers the work we run on our side. A separate clipping budget pays for the views themselves, priced on cost per thousand views. On the clipping budget there are two models. Mass clipping uses a large pool of creators for high volume and saturation: launches, music, big moments. An in-house clipper team is smaller and managed, built for conversion when you want measurable action. Either way we set a rate per thousand views and cap the payout per clip, so spend stays predictable. You only pay for views that clear review. We size the retainer and the budget to your goal on the call.

A campaign runs in two phases. Within the first 72 hours, clips are live and views are moving. Then come the weeks after, where the real value sits, because clips don't expire. They keep getting found, so reach compounds long after launch. Most clients run a defined pilot first, long enough to read the numbers honestly, then decide whether to scale. We'll tell you straight what length fits your goal.

Two sides: distribution and talent. On distribution, the Paul American launch passed 11M views, Iman Gadzhi did 42M across a 90-day amplification, and Luke Belmar's Capital Club runs 200M a month. On music, Kojo Blak and Fantana at a $1.30 CPM — plus brand work like Walmart. On talent, we span music, sport, fashion and culture.
